We report a pre-registered benchmark of an entanglement-forged H_4 ground-state calculation on IonQ Forte Enterprise 1, a live trapped-ion processor. The analysis was frozen and publicly committed before any hardware data existed; the run was a single submission of 81 circuits at 1100 shots with device debiasing explicitly disabled. Two findings are in tension and we report both. A three-parameter analytic noise model, fixed in advance, predicted the uncorrected energy error to within 1% (23.84 against 24.06 kcal/mol) and the postselection retention to 0.4%. The correction built on that same model then failed: the oracle-free estimator returned 3.46 kcal/mol against 0.83 predicted, a factor of 4.16, missing chemical accuracy by 3.5 x despite removing 85% of the raw error. Goodness of fit degraded from chi^2/dof 2 on every noise model to 5.93 on hardware, in all 20 bootstrap resamples, so the failure was detectable from the hardware counts alone without reference to the known answer. Decomposing all 732 residuals by measurement group and Pauli weight localises the discrepancy to high-weight XY strings carrying the largest corrections and to a signed offset in one group. Refitting a single correction-magnitude parameter, blind to the reference energy, shows the model under-corrects by 1.8 x ; repairing this removes only a third of the excess and makes the energy slightly worse, so fit quality and accuracy are decoupled. We conclude that a noise model calibrated on raw observables must not be assumed predictive of corrected ones, that bootstrap resampling understates submission-to-submission variance, and that at 1100 shots the oracle-free estimator is shot-noise limited rather than noise limited, so mitigation alone cannot close the gap.
